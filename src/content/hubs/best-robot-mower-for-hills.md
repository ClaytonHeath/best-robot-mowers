If the lawn has real grade, **drive type and published slope %** matter more than brand storytelling. This hub curates published listings on [Best Robot Lawn Mowers](/) that the manufacturer rates for steeper work areas — especially all-wheel-drive (AWD) SKUs. It is not a ranked “best hillside mower” scorecard and not a substitute for measuring your own bank.

Coverage and slope on each card are what the manufacturer publishes. Wet grass, loose thatch, drop-offs, and driveway transitions are outside those lab-style figures.

## Slope % vs degrees (and what the number actually means)

Robot mower specs almost always use **percent grade**, not degrees.

| Approximate relationship | |
| --- | --- |
| 17° ≈ **30%** | Common small-yard / 2WD ceiling (e.g. many Worx / Navimow i-series listings) |
| 24° ≈ **45%** | Mid catalog (many Anthbot / Husqvarna 2WD / RockNeo) |
| 27° ≈ **50%** | e.g. Navimow X390 work-area; ECOVACS GOAT work-area |
| ~35° ≈ **70%** | Husqvarna AWD in-lawn; Yarbo max climb |
| ~38–39° ≈ **80%** | Dreame A3 AWD series; Mammotion LUBA 3 AWD |
| 40° ≈ **84%** | Navimow X430 published max climb |

**Work-area max vs boundary / edge:** Several listings publish a **lower** limit at the boundary than inside the lawn. Examples from the catalog:

- [Husqvarna Automower 435 iQ AWD](/mowers/husqvarna-automower-435-iq-awd/): **70%** in-lawn / **50%** at the boundary
- [Husqvarna Automower 435X AWD](/mowers/husqvarna-automower-435x-awd/): **70%** in-lawn / **50%** at the wire
- [Husqvarna Automower 440 iQ](/mowers/husqvarna-automower-440-iq/) and [410 iQ](/mowers/husqvarna-automower-410-iq/): **45%** in-lawn / **15%** at the boundary
- [Segway Navimow i105](/mowers/segway-navimow-i105/) / [i110](/mowers/segway-navimow-i110/): **30%** work area / **10%** at the boundary
- [ECOVACS GOAT A3000 LiDAR PRO](/mowers/ecovacs-goat-a3000-lidar-pro/): **50%** work area / **20%** virtual-boundary slope

If the steep part of your property is the **mapped edge** (retaining wall, bank along the sidewalk), the boundary number is the one that breaks the deal — not the headline climb % on the marketing banner.

**Driveways and drop-offs:** A slope rating is not permission to send the mower down a paved grade, over a curb cut, or along an unfenced drop. Listings do not replace site judgment. Keep no-go zones and physical barriers where a slip would matter.

**Wet grass:** Manufacturer climb figures assume traction. After rain, the same 50–80% bank can become a slide. Favor AWD when grade is the reason you are shopping; still treat wet days as operator judgment, not a warranty feature.

## Honest limits on manufacturer claims

1. **Headline % is a maximum, not a daily operating target.** Listings already frame slope as a published ceiling.
2. **Boundary slope is often lower** than in-lawn slope (Husqvarna iQ, Navimow i-series, GOAT virtual boundary). Map the steep edge carefully.
3. **Coverage and slope are independent gates.** LUBA 3 AWD 1500 has 80% slope but only **0.37 acre**. X430 has 84% and **1 acre**. Matching one number and ignoring the other is how returns happen.
4. **Wire-free still needs sensors that work.** RTK wants sky (or network RTK where offered). LiDAR/vision want a clear view of the scene. Hills with dense tree canopy can stress either stack — that is site-specific, not a catalog field.
5. **IP ratings are weather sealing, not “wash the underside after a mud climb.”** Example: 435 iQ AWD is IPX4 with an explicit no-hose-wash underside note on the listing.

## How to choose from this hub

1. Measure the **steepest continuous grade inside the grass** you expect the mower to climb while cutting.
2. Separately check grade **at the planned boundary**.
3. Filter by **coverage** so the acre rating still fits.
4. Prefer **AWD** listings when grade is the reason you are here.
5. Open 2–3 listing pages and compare caveats side by side on the [homepage](/) or [wire-free hub](/wire-free/).
