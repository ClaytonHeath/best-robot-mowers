## What it is

The Worx Landroid Vision Cloud 4WD WR341 is the quarter-acre entry model in Worx's wire-free, four-wheel-drive Vision Cloud 4WD family on the official US store. This listing covers only the **¼ Acre / WR341** configuration: partNumber and SKU **WR341**, catalog entry id **3074457345616683942** (under parent product **3074457345616683236**).

It shares a configurable PDP with three Vision Cloud 4WD siblings: the already-listed [WR342](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr342/) (½ acre, $2,299.99), the already-listed [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/) (1 acre, $2,999.99), and **WR346** (1½ acre, $3,699.99). It is also a different chassis from the two-wheel-drive Landroid Vision Cloud [WR310.1](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-wr310/) (¼ acre, $1,199.99, 30% slope) and its 2WD siblings WR320.1 and WR340.

From the official US specifications block for partNumber WR341 and the WR341/WR342 owner's manual on Worx's CDN (checked **28 Oct 2026**):

- Recommended lawn size: **0.25 acre** (US spec table); manual cutting area **1/4 acre (1000 m²)**
- Navigation: **RTK Cloud** with a **stereo** camera (Vision AI) and **V-SLAM**; no perimeter wire
- Maximum slope: **84% (40°)** on both the US spec table and the manual; the manual caps it at **15°** when the included Cut-to-Zero edge module is fitted
- Drive: **4-wheel drive** with a terrain-adaptive body and front steering
- Cutting width / diameter: **8.7 in** (22 cm), **3 blades**, self-leveling blade disc, **2400 rpm** no-load speed
- Cutting height: **1.57–3.54 in** (40–90 mm), electric adjustment, six steps (`1.57–1.97–2.36–2.76–3.15–3.54 in`) in the manual
- Battery and charger: Worx PowerShare **20 V, 4 Ah** Li-ion (**WA3674**); charge time **80 min** on the included **90 W / 3 A** charger (**WA3752**, `120 V~ 60 Hz, 90 W`, output `20 V, 3.0 A`)
- Connectivity: **Bluetooth** and **Wi-Fi** (2.4 GHz); unlike WR342 (which includes RadioLink) and WR344/WR346 (which include 4G), WR341 includes neither RadioLink nor 4G in the box
- Weight: **37.3 lbs** with battery on the US spec table; top-level catalog attribute **39.9 lbs**; manual machinery weight **41.9 lb (19 kg)**
- Dimensions: **26.1 × 18.9 × 11.5 in** (depth × width × height)
- Water protection: **IPX6** (manual); the US spec table marks a hose-washable deck
- Also listed: rain sensor, Find My Landroid module, Cut-to-Zero module, multi-zone management, over-the-air and USB updates

`coverageAcres` is that **0.25 acre** recommended lawn size, not a daily rate.

## Who it's for

Homeowners with up to **a quarter acre of mowable grass** that has steep banks, rough transitions, or multiple zones where the two-wheel-drive [WR310.1](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-wr310/) (rated for 30% slopes) is not enough. Like the half-acre [WR342](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr342/) and 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/), the WR341 uses satellite RTK with cloud corrections instead of a reference antenna pole in the yard, paired with stereo vision and V-SLAM for mapping and tree cover.

Check two site requirements before buying:

1. **RTK Cloud coverage:** Run the **"Is RTK available at my address?"** tool on the Worx product page to confirm Worx's cloud correction network covers your property.
2. **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi reach across the lawn:** The WR341 relies on **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and Bluetooth** out of the box. It does **not** include the [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/)'s built-in 4G module, and unlike the [WR342](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr342/) ($2,299.99), it does **not** include the **RadioLink** range-extender module in the box (the WR341/WR342 manual marks RadioLink as standard only with WR342). If your home Wi-Fi drops at the far end of the yard, the $300 step up to WR342 is the safer buy.

Skip it if your mowable area exceeds 0.25 acre, if RTK Cloud does not cover your address, if your yard has Wi-Fi dead spots where WR342's included RadioLink or WR344's 4G would matter, or if you want a cheaper quarter-acre 80%-slope AWD LiDAR mower such as the [Dreame A3 AWD 1000](/mowers/dreame-a3-awd-1000/) ($1,399.99).

## Standout details

- **How WR341 ($1,999.99) differs from WR342 ($2,299.99) and WR344 ($2,999.99):** All three share the same 4WD chassis, **84% (40°)** slope rating, **8.7 in** 3-blade deck, **1.57–3.54 in** electric height range, and **Cut-to-Zero** edge module. Stepping down to the WR341 saves **$300** vs the ½-acre [WR342](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr342/), but cuts the rated area from ½ acre to **¼ acre**, drops the battery from **5 Ah (WA3675)** to **4 Ah (WA3674)** (**80 min** vs 100 min charge on the same **90 W / 3 A** WA3752 charger), and omits the **RadioLink** module that comes standard with WR342. Stepping up to the 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/) ($2,999.99) adds 1-acre capacity, built-in **4G**, the **FiatLux** headlight, and a faster **190 W / 5 A** charger (60 min charge on a 5 Ah pack).
- **Slope and the Cut-to-Zero catch:** The spec table and manual both rate the 4WD drive system for **84% (40°)** slopes, with a terrain-adaptive chassis and front steering. However, the manual's technical data carries the same footnote as WR342 and WR344: **if the mower is equipped with the Cut-to-Zero module, Worx does not recommend operating it on slopes greater than 15°**. The Cut-to-Zero module (**110 mm** width; three manual heights of **2.6, 3.0, and 3.4 in** / 65, 75, and 85 mm) comes in the box, so steep yards have to choose between close border trimming and the full slope rating.
- **In the box:** Both the US `What's Included` tab and the WR341 column of the WR341/WR342 manual list: Worx Landroid Vision Cloud WR341 robotic mower ×1, charging tower ×1, charging base plate ×1, power supply / charger (**WA3752**) ×1, **20 V 4.0 Ah** PowerShare battery (**WA3674**) ×1, Cut-to-Zero module ×1, spare blades ×8, hex key ×1, lawn screws ×8, magnetic No-Go Line **19.7 ft (6 m)**, and lawn stakes / pegs ×10.
- **Warranty and returns:** 3-year warranty and 30-day money-back guarantee on the official US listing.

## Honest verdict

At **$1,999.99**, the WR341 brings Worx's **84%-slope four-wheel-drive** chassis and **RTK Cloud + stereo vision + V-SLAM** navigation down to the quarter-acre tier for **$300 less** than the half-acre [WR342](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr342/) ($2,299.99) and **$1,000 less** than the 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/) ($2,999.99). Compared with Worx's two-wheel-drive quarter-acre [WR310.1](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-wr310/) ($1,199.99, 30% slope), you are paying $800 more specifically for four-wheel drive, steep-slope traction, and the included Cut-to-Zero edge module.

The catch is that saving $300 versus the WR342 costs more than just software acreage: you drop from a 5 Ah to a **4 Ah** battery and lose the **RadioLink** module in the box, leaving the WR341 dependent on **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi** reach while still lacking built-in 4G. You also face stiff quarter-acre AWD competition from the LiDAR-guided [Dreame A3 AWD 1000](/mowers/dreame-a3-awd-1000/) ($1,399.99, 80% slope) and must live with Worx's **8.7-inch** deck and **15°** slope limit whenever the Cut-to-Zero module is attached. Buy the WR341 only if your lawn is truly within **0.25 acre**, RTK Cloud and home Wi-Fi cover the whole yard, and you want Worx's 4WD platform at its lowest US entry price.
