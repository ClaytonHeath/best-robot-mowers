## What it is

The Worx Landroid Vision Cloud 4WD WR342 is the half-acre model in Worx's wire-free, four-wheel-drive Vision Cloud 4WD family on the official US store. This listing covers only the **½ Acre / WR342** configuration: partNumber and SKU **WR342**, catalog entry id **3074457345616683943** (under parent product **3074457345616683236**).

It shares a configurable PDP with three Vision Cloud 4WD siblings: **WR341** (¼ acre, $1,999.99), the already-listed [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/) (1 acre, $2,999.99), and **WR346** (1½ acre, $3,699.99). It is also a different platform from the two-wheel-drive Landroid Vision Cloud [WR310.1](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-wr310/) (¼ acre, 30% slope) and its 2WD siblings WR320.1 and WR340.

## Who it's for

Homeowners with up to about **half an acre of mowable grass** that includes steep banks, rough transitions, or multiple zones where a 30%-slope two-wheel-drive mower would slip. Like the 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/), the WR342 uses satellite RTK with corrections from Worx's cloud instead of a reference antenna pole in the yard, paired with stereo vision and V-SLAM for mapping and tree cover.

Before buying, run the **"Is RTK available at my address?"** check on the Worx product page, because RTK Cloud coverage has to reach your property. Unlike the 1-acre WR344 and 1½-acre WR346, the WR342 does **not** include built-in 4G cellular connectivity. Instead, it relies on **2.4 GHz Wi-Fi** and an included **RadioLink** module that links the mower to the charging station for RTK positioning data and yard-wide coverage. Both the charging station and RadioLink need a working Wi-Fi connection back to your home network.

Skip it if your mowable lawn is closer to a full acre (step up to the [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/)), if your property lacks Wi-Fi reach and you specifically want the WR344's built-in 4G, if RTK Cloud does not cover your address, or if you want a wider deck than **8.7 inches**.

## Standout details

- **How WR342 differs from WR344 ($2,999.99) and WR341 ($1,999.99):** Against the 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/), the WR342 keeps the same 4WD chassis, 84% slope rating, 8.7 in deck, and 20 V 5 Ah battery (**WA3675**), for **$700 less**. What you give up are the 1-acre software/capacity tier, built-in **4G**, the **FiatLux** headlight (included on WR344/WR346 only), and the faster **190 W / 5 A** charger (WR342 uses a **90 W / 3 A** charger and takes **100 minutes** to charge instead of 60 minutes, while adding the **RadioLink** module). Against the ¼-acre **WR341** ($1,999.99), spending $300 more on WR342 doubles the rated area (½ vs ¼ acre), steps the battery up from **4 Ah (WA3674)** to **5 Ah (WA3675)**, and adds **RadioLink** in the box (the manual marks RadioLink as standard only with WR342).

The Cut-to-Zero module (**110 mm** width; three manual heights of **2.6, 3.0, and 3.4 in** / 65, 75, and 85 mm) comes in the box, so steep yards have to choose between close border trimming and the full slope rating.

## Honest verdict

At **$2,299.99**, the WR342 is the sweet spot in Worx's Vision Cloud 4WD lineup if your lawn is half an acre or less. It undercuts the 1-acre [WR344](/mowers/worx-landroid-vision-cloud-4wd-wr344/) by **$700** while keeping the same four-wheel-drive chassis, **84%** slope rating, **RTK Cloud + stereo vision + V-SLAM** navigation, and **20 V 5 Ah** PowerShare battery, and it improves on the ¼-acre WR341 ($1,999.99) by doubling the coverage rating, upgrading the battery from 4 Ah to 5 Ah, and including the **RadioLink** module in the box.

The catches are the same physical limits as the rest of the series plus two WR342-specific trade-offs: you get **Wi-Fi + RadioLink** rather than built-in **4G**, and the included **90 W** charger takes **100 minutes** to refill the 5 Ah pack instead of the WR344's 60 minutes. Add the **8.7-inch** cutting width, the requirement that RTK Cloud cover your address, and the manual's **15°** slope warning whenever the Cut-to-Zero module is mounted, and the buyer call is clear: verify RTK Cloud coverage first, and plan to take the edge module off if you are buying this 4WD mower for steep banks.
