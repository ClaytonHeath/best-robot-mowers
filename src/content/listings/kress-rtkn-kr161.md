## What it is

## Who it's for

This fits a lawn whose **mowable area is within 0.25 acre**, where you want virtual boundaries instead of buried wire, and where you do not want to site a fixed RTK base station on the property. Kress's page says RTKⁿ uses the brand's network of base stations so the mower gets correction data without extra yard hardware. The published work-area slope is **40%**.

Plan the install before you buy. There is **no perimeter wire**, but the packing list still includes a **charging base**, **charging tower**, **RTK antenna**, antenna wrench, **magnetic strip**, staples, and nails. You still place the charging hardware and mount the included antenna. **4G** is marked on the US spec table for this SKU.

Skip it if the grass area is past a quarter acre: step up the Mission RTKⁿ line (KR172 and above) or another brand. Skip it if you need a published AWD rating or a slope at the 70%+ class — this page's maximum is **40%** inside the work surface. No separate boundary-slope percent is published beyond that work-surface row, so `slopePercent` is **40**.

## Honest verdict

At **$1,499.99** on sale from **$2,099.99**, the KR161 is the small end of Kress's US Mission RTKⁿ line: **up to 0.25 acre**, network **RTKⁿ** without a yard base station, **no perimeter wire**, a **7.87-inch** cut, and a **40%** work-area slope rating, with **4G** marked on the spec table.
