## What it is

The Kress EyePilot RTKⁿ KR276 is the largest wire-free mower in Kress's two-wheel-drive EyePilot range, sold on the official US store at kress.com/en-us. This listing covers the **KR276** only: WooCommerce product id **1301153**, SKU **KR276**. Kress names it the "EyePilot RTKⁿ 2.5 acre robotic lawn mower with ZeroTrim with integrated 4G".

It navigates with **RTKⁿ**: satellite positioning with correction data sent over Wi-Fi or 4G from Kress's reference-station network, so there is no base station to install in the yard. **EyePilot vision-based awareness** handles obstacles, and Kress says a redundant positioning setup keeps it accurate when the satellite signal is weak. The **ZeroTrim** system cuts close to edges.

The "2.5 acre" in the name is a 48-hour figure. Kress's key features list up to **1.5 acres in 24 hours** and 2.5 acres in 48 hours. (The page description also calls it a "1.5 acre robotic mower".) It is not the ½-acre [KR272](/mowers/kress-eyepilot-rtkn-kr272/), not the EyePilot 4×4 models ([KR282](/mowers/kress-eyepilot-4x4-rtkn-kr282/), KR284, KR286), and not the Mission RTKⁿ line ([KR161](/mowers/kress-rtkn-kr161/), KR172–KR174).

## Who it's for

- Large lawns: about **1.5 acres per day**, or up to **2.5 acres** if it can run across two days.
- Mostly gentle ground. It is rated for **40%** slopes and is rear-wheel drive.
- Owners who want edges trimmed less (**ZeroTrim**) and obstacles seen by camera rather than bumped.
- Yards without a good spot for an RTK base station, but with Wi-Fi or 4G coverage for the RTKⁿ corrections.

## Honest verdict

At **$3,599.99** (23 Oct 2026), the KR276 is Kress's capacity pick. It has network **RTKⁿ** with **camera obstacle awareness**, no wire or base station, **ZeroTrim** edging, a 4-blade **9.5 in** deck, **IPX6** and **4G** with no ongoing fee. Its **1.5 acres a day** is three times the KR272's half acre, for $1,100 more.

The catch is the same-price alternative. Kress sells the EyePilot 4×4 **KR284** for the same **$3,599.99**. That model is rated for about an acre but has all-wheel drive for steep ground, while the KR276 stays **rear-wheel drive** at **40%**. The "2.5 acre" headline also depends on a 48-hour cycle. Buy the KR276 for a big, mostly flat lawn. If the yard has real hills, the KR284 is the better use of the same money.
